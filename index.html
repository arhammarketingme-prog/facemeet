Privacy Center

Plain-language answers about what Nexus stores, what's encrypted, and what's public.

What's public

Your profile (username, display name, bio, channel stats), your posts, comments, likes, follows, community memberships, and polls you create are all public by design — this is a public social platform.

What's encrypted

Direct messages are encrypted in your browser (AES-GCM, with the key wrapped for each recipient using RSA-OAEP) before they ever leave your device. The server stores only ciphertext — Nexus staff cannot read your message contents.

Honest limitation: your private key currently lives only in this browser's local storage. If you clear browser data or switch devices without backing it up, you'll lose access to old messages. There's no server-side password recovery for this, by design — that's what "the server never sees plaintext" means in practice.

What's NOT stored

We don't store plaintext message contents, and we practice data minimization elsewhere too — no invasive location tracking, no third-party ad trackers embedded in the page.

What we don't claim

Nexus is privacy-first, not a legal guarantee. We don't claim to be "legally certified," and we don't claim "100% anonymity" — your account is tied to your email, and public content is, well, public.
